## Executive Summary

Pronto is India's fastest-growing on-demand house help platform with 500K+ homes served across 11 cities, $100M valuation, and $25M Series A funding. This playbook identifies **20 high-impact growth opportunities** across both demand and supply sides of Pronto's two-sided marketplace, with a prioritized 90-day experiment plan to drive sustainable, compounding growth.

**Key Findings:**
- Pronto has strong product-market fit on **both sides** (4.5★ demand, Pro retention strong) but underutilized network effects
- **Supply-side growth is the #1 opportunity** — Pro Mentor program could match Urban Company's footprint with 1/10th the resources (Lyft playbook)

- **Liquidity is the core marketplace metric** — Pro density per neighborhood determines when demand helps vs. hurts
- Massive opportunity in referral programs, founder-led social, and community building
- First booking should be **FREE** (not discounted) — treat as marketing spend, not revenue loss

## Part 4: Marketplace Health & Liquidity (NEW in v2)

### 4.1 Liquidity: Pronto's #1 Marketplace Metric

**Definition:** Liquidity = the fill rate of intentful demand. When a customer opens the Pronto app intending to book house help, what percentage actually complete a booking?

**Pronto's Liquidity Metric:**
> "Weekly completed bookings where the customer got a Pro within 15 minutes AND rated the experience 4+ stars"

This replaces "Weekly Active Bookers" as the North Star Metric because it captures:
- **Demand** (completed bookings)
- **Supply quality** (15-minute arrival)
- **Experience quality** (4+ star rating)

### 4.2 Pro Density Thresholds

For Pronto's 15-minute promise to hold, each neighborhood needs a minimum density of available Pros. Below this threshold, adding more demand actually **degrades** the customer experience.

**Estimated Thresholds (to be validated with data):**

| Neighborhood Type | Min Pros Needed | Target Fill Rate |
|-------------------|----------------|-----------------|
| High-density (apartments, gated communities) | 10 Pros per 1,000 households | 95%+ |
| Medium-density (mixed residential) | 15 Pros per 1,000 households | 90%+ |
| Low-density (independent houses, outskirts) | 20 Pros per 1,000 households | 85%+ |

### 4.3 Market Health Dashboard

| Metric | Definition | Target | Frequency |
|--------|-----------|--------|-----------|
| **Fill Rate** | % of booking requests fulfilled within 15 min | 95%+ | Daily |
| **Pro Utilization** | % of available Pros with at least 1 booking/day | 70%+ | Daily |
| **Pro Density** | Pros available per 1,000 households per neighborhood | Varies | Weekly |
| **Customer Wait Time** | Average time from booking request to Pro arrival | <15 min | Daily |
| **Pro Churn Rate** | % of Pros who stop accepting bookings per month | <10% | Monthly |
| **Customer NPS** | Net Promoter Score | >70 | Monthly |
| **Repeat Booking Rate** | % of customers who book again within 30 days | 45%+ | Monthly |

### 4.4 Supply-Demand Interaction Model

**Key Insight:** Adding more demand in a low-liquidity area **degrades** the experience. Pronto should only scale demand in neighborhoods where Pro density is above the threshold.

```
IF Pro density > threshold:
    → Scale demand (referrals, ads, partnerships)
    → Result: More bookings, better Pro utilization, higher earnings

IF Pro density < threshold:
    → Scale supply first (Pro recruitment, mentor program)
    → Result: Better fill rate, faster arrival, higher retention
    → THEN scale demand
```

Each experiment is scored on:
- **Impact** (1-10): Potential to move key metrics
- **Confidence** (1-10): Likelihood of success based on data

### Experiment 1: Structured Referral Program
**ICE Score: 9.0** (Impact: 10, Confidence: 9, Ease: 8)

**Hypothesis:**  
Implementing a double-sided referral program (₹100 for referrer, ₹100 for referee) will drive 20% of new user acquisition within 90 days.

**Why This Works:**
- House help is a high-trust category (people trust friends/family recommendations)
- Urban Company and BookMyBai lack structured referral mechanics
- Pronto's 500K+ user base is a massive untapped distribution channel
- Double-sided rewards increase conversion by 2-3x vs. single-sided

**Implementation:**
1. **Referral Mechanics:**
   - Referrer gets ₹100 credit after referee completes first booking
   - Referee gets ₹100 off first booking

   - Share via WhatsApp, SMS, email

2. **Activation Triggers:**
   - Post-booking prompt: "Love Pronto? Refer a friend and both get ₹100"
   - In-app referral dashboard with tracking
   - Push notification 24h after booking: "Your neighbor could use Pronto too"

3. **Gamification:**
   - Leaderboard: "Top referrers this month"
   - Milestones: "Refer 5 friends, get a free booking"
   - Social proof: "X people joined via your link"

**Success Metrics:**
- Referral signup rate (target: 15% of new users)
- Referral conversion rate (target: 40% of referred signups book)
- Cost per acquisition via referral (target: <₹150)
- Viral coefficient (target: >0.3)

**Timeline:** 2 weeks to build, 4 weeks to test

### Experiment 10: Subscription Plans — Pronto Plus (Updated in v2)
**ICE Score: 6.0** (Impact: 8, Confidence: 5, Ease: 5)

**v2 Change:** ICE score decreased from 6.3 to 6.0 based on Casey Winters' warning about consumer subscription economics. Added unit economics model requirement and "reverse free trial" approach from Albert Cheng.

**Hypothesis:**  
Launching "Pronto Plus" subscription with a reverse free trial (let users experience premium features in-context) will convert 15% of power users and increase LTV by 3x.

**Unit Economics Model (Required Before Launch):**
- Expected annual retention: Must be >60% to be profitable
- Cannibalization risk: What % of subscription users would have booked at full price anyway?
- Break-even timeline: How many months until subscription revenue exceeds discount cost?
- If retention <50%, this is a money-losing proposition

**Implementation:**
1. **Reverse Free Trial (Albert Cheng's Grammarly approach):**
   - Let users experience priority booking once for free
   - Assign a dedicated Pro for their 3rd booking
   - Show them what their "Pronto Plus" savings dashboard would look like
   - Let them taste premium before asking them to pay

2. **Plan Structure:**
   - Pronto Plus: ₹999/month (4 cleanings, save ₹400)
   - Pronto Pro: ₹1,999/month (8 cleanings, save ₹800)
   - Pronto Family: ₹2,999/month (12 cleanings + laundry + utensils)

3. **Benefits:**
   - Priority booking (skip the queue)
   - Dedicated Pro (same person every time)
   - Free rescheduling
   - Exclusive support line

**Success Metrics:**
- Subscription adoption (target: 15% of power users)
- Subscriber LTV (target: 3x non-subscribers)
- Churn rate (target: <10% monthly)

## Part 6: Explore/Exploit Framework (NEW in v2)

**Expert Source:** Albert Cheng (Duolingo, Grammarly, Chess.com)

### 6.1 The Framework

Growth work oscillates between two modes:

**EXPLORE** — Find the right mountain
- Run many small experiments across different areas
- Look for counterintuitive behavioral patterns
- Cast a wide net
- Accept high failure rate (80% of experiments fail)

**EXPLOIT** — Climb the mountain fast
- When you find a breakout win, swarm around it
- Propagate the insight across other experiments
- Scale what works, kill what doesn't
- Articulate the hypothesis clearly so the team can rally

### 6.2 How Pronto Will Apply This

**First 30 Days: EXPLORE Mode**
- Run all 20 experiments in parallel (small versions)
- Measure effect sizes and statistical significance
- Look for counterintuitive patterns (like Chess.com's game review positivity insight)
- Identify the top 3 breakout wins

**Days 31-60: EXPLOIT Mode**
- Double down on top 3 wins
- Propagate insights across other experiments
- Scale winning experiments to full rollout
- Kill underperforming experiments

**Days 61-90: RE-EXPLORE**
- With learnings from exploit phase, explore new areas
- Test variations of winning experiments
- Look for second-order effects
- Plan next quarter's explore/exploit cycle

### 6.3 Breakout Win Identification

A "breakout win" is defined as:
- Statistically significant (p < 0.05)
- Effect size > 20% improvement on target metric
- Scalable (can be applied across cities/services)
- Compounding (strengthens network effects)

### 6.4 Learning Propagation

When a breakout win is found:
1. **Document the insight** clearly (what worked, why, for whom)
2. **Identify adjacent applications** (where else can this insight apply?)
3. **Run propagation experiments** (apply the insight to other areas)
4. **Share learnings** across the growth team

**Example (from Chess.com):**
- Breakout: Positivity after losses grew game reviews 25% and subscriptions 20%
- Propagation: Applied same insight to puzzles, lessons, and other features
- Result: Company-wide uplift in engagement

## Part 9: "Don't Do This" Section (NEW in v2)

**Expert Source:** Dan Hockenmaier (Faire, Thumbtack)

### What Pronto Should NOT Do

Dan's gardener metaphor: "Marketplaces are gardens, not construction sites. Every decision has second and third-order consequences."

1. **DON'T expand to new cities until liquidity in existing cities hits the threshold.**
   - Adding cities with low Pro density will degrade the experience and generate negative word-of-mouth.
   - Rule: Only expand when 80%+ of neighborhoods in current cities are "Green" on the supply density dashboard.

2. **DON'T launch subscriptions before proving repeat booking behavior.**
   - Pronto Plus should only launch after 30%+ of users are booking 2+ times per month organically.
   - Otherwise, you're discounting behavior that would have happened anyway.

3. **DON'T run corporate partnerships in neighborhoods with low Pro density.**
   - Corporate partnerships create demand spikes. If supply can't handle it, you'll destroy the experience.
   - Rule: Only partner with companies in "Green" neighborhoods.

4. **DON'T hard-filter Pros by specialization or premium tier.**
   - Ben Lauzier's Thumbtack smoke machine lesson: preference checkboxes that seem harmless can filter out 95% of supply.
   - Use ranking, not hard filtering. Let preferences affect sort order, not availability.

5. **DON'T run 12 experiments simultaneously without sequencing.**
   - Each experiment has second-order effects on the marketplace ecosystem.
   - Sequence experiments: supply-side first, then demand-side, then innovation.

6. **DON'T optimize for user count over share of wallet.**
   - Dan: "If you could grow GMV 10% by getting 10% more customers or by getting 10% more of current customers' wallet, take the latter."
   - Depth beats breadth. Focus on getting existing users to use Pronto for ALL their house help needs.
